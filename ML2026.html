Course Description

This course introduces the mathematical foundations necessary for understanding and applying machine learning techniques. The guiding thread is the notion of a data distribution: we begin by sampling data from distributions (Gaussians and mixtures of Gaussians), build graphs from the sampled data, and study random walks on those graphs. The linear algebra of the course — matrices as operators, linear systems, eigenvalues, the spectral theorem, and the SVD — is developed exactly when each random-walk phenomenon demands it. The tools are then applied to spectral clustering and principal component analysis, and the course culminates with an introduction to generative models: we learn to sample new data points from a learned distribution by reversing a noising Markov chain (diffusion models), with all computations in closed form for Gaussian mixtures.

Prerequisites

Basic knowledge of linear algebra, calculus, and probability.

Course Format

The course consists of lectures with in-class quizzes, two midterms, and a final exam. There are no graded homework assignments: instead, practice notebooks (Python) are released ahead of each quiz, and each quiz tests the same skills as its practice notebook. Students are encouraged to collaborate on practice notebooks; quizzes and exams are individual work.

Grading

  • In-class quizzes: 30% (drop the lowest two)
  • Midterm Exams: 2 × 20%
  • Final Exam: 30%
  • If your final exam score is higher than your lower midterm score, the final automatically replaces that midterm.
  • Students with a clear A from quizzes and midterms alone may opt out of the final exam with the instructor's approval.
